01

A physical platform for sensor testing

A physiological phantom reproduces selected properties of the body so a sensor can be examined under controlled conditions. In the OBSL platform, anatomical phantoms and pulsatile flow provide a setting for testing wearable sensing devices. The lab’s wider platform includes control of pressure, temperature, and motion.

The photographs show a physical arm fixture with devices under test. The enclosure image is a lab CAD rendering. These images describe the research environment; the full platform is the work of a broader research and engineering team.

02

My firmware and acquisition contribution

My work supports biomedical phantom pressure-monitoring experiments using Arduino Portenta H7, analog and digital pressure sensors, ADC/DAC interfaces, level shifting, and Python test software. The focus is connecting hardware to real-time acquisition and making the resulting waveforms available for inspection.

The firmware and host tool are parts of the same engineering workflow. A sensor interface brings the signal into the embedded system; host-side acquisition and plotting make it possible to review waveform behavior during experimental validation.

03

Verifying the path from pressure to data

My responsibilities include waveform verification and root-cause debugging around timing, interfaces, and signal acquisition. Oscilloscopes and logic analyzers help inspect electrical behavior, while Python tools provide a view of the data arriving at the host.

The aim is to make experimental behavior observable. A pressure reading needs a clear path through its electrical interface, conversion stage, firmware, and host representation before it can be interpreted within an experiment.

04

What the walkthrough explains

The optional view below uses synthetic inlet and outlet traces. Heart rate changes pulse spacing, while the systolic and diastolic controls change the displayed pressure range. The outlet trace is offset to illustrate that two measurement channels can differ in time.

The traces are a visual explanation of pulse timing, pressure range, and acquisition channels. They complement the project’s firmware, sensor integration, and validation story, with synthetic values generated locally in the browser.
